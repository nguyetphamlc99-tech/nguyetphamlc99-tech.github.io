Explore with care

Start with questions that help people.

Good nonprofit analysis connects useful evidence to a mission. These prompts can guide a project before you choose a chart or model.

01 / RELATIONSHIPS

Are people coming back?

Compare first-time and returning donors over consistent time periods. Look for changes, not just totals.

02 / CAMPAIGNS

What invites participation?

Compare campaign timing, audience, and giving patterns while being clear about what the data cannot explain.

03 / STEWARDSHIP

Where can follow-up improve?

Use aggregate patterns to plan timely, respectful communication and recognize donor preferences.

A project toolkit

Useful analysis is clear about its limits.

As you add a real dataset, document where it came from, what each row represents, and which fields are missing or approximate.

Define the unitIs one row a donor, a gift, or a campaign-day?
Check the time windowAre you comparing complete and comparable periods?
Separate association from causeA pattern can suggest a question without proving why it happened.
Explain the measureState how you define retention, average gift, and campaign success.

Responsible data

Protect trust while learning.

Donor data describes people. Handle it with the same care your organization gives its relationships.

✓

Keep personal details private. Do not publish donor names, emails, addresses, or account identifiers.

✓

Use only data you are allowed to use. Follow the dataset’s terms and your organization’s policies.

✓

Prefer aggregate results. Share trends that support decisions without exposing an individual donor.

✓

Make assumptions visible. Label sample values, exclusions, and limitations in every analysis.
